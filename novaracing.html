NovaRacing

Villanova's Formula SAE team designs and builds an open-wheel formula race car from scratch every year. I work as an Aerodynamics Subsystem Engineer on the Chassis System, moving from composite manufacturing on the 2026 car (VU17) to front wing design for the 2027 car (VU18).

Role Aerodynamics Subsystem Engineer, Chassis System Team NovaRacing Formula SAE, Villanova University Timeline Nov. 2025 – Present
VU17 Composite Structures Manufacturing

Overview

In my freshman spring on NovaRacing, I manufactured 20+ carbon-fiber parts for VU17’s aerodynamic package: the front and rear wings, undertray, nosecone, sidepods, and body panels. Schedule delays compressed manufacturing into three months, and we finished the package in time for competition.

I attended competition and joined aerodynamic design judging with a senior capstone teammate. Limited testing left the package’s full-car performance unvalidated, and two engine failures cut our event short. The judges’ takeaway: set downforce, drag, balance, and cooling targets early, and let simulation and testing drive design before manufacturing.

Made possible by the support and guidance of the senior capstone team (pictured in the gallery), and the help of Luke Gustafson ’27, Matthew Parker ’27, Luke VanEmburgh ’27, and Randall Preson ’29.

Mold Preparation

Every part started as a mold, 10+ in all. The 3D-printed PLA molds were sanded from 60 to 4000 grit to remove transferred print lines and give the carbon the best aerodynamic surface finish possible.

Larger molds began as MDF sheet stock glued into blocks, then cut on the CNC router. They went through the same sanding procedure, then were sealed with polyurethane and coated in PVA release film before layup.

Wet Layup

Carbon-fiber plies were laid into the prepared molds by hand and wetted out with epoxy, working resin through each layer before the next went down. The body panels were done together as one large layup, then vacuum-bagged to consolidate the plies while they cured.

The focus was even resin coverage and tight conformity to the mold, keeping dry spots and trapped air out of the laminate.

Resin Infusion

The nosecone was manufactured using resin infusion, with dry carbon fiber laid into the mold, sealed under a vacuum bag, and infused with a 10:3 resin-to-hardener mixture under vacuum.

The process helped control excess resin and compact the fiber layers, producing a very lightweight, rigid nosecone. The main challenges were preventing microbubbles from becoming trapped within the laminate and achieving even resin flow across the entire mold surface.

Post-Processing & Assembly

After curing, the carbon-fiber components were trimmed, sanded, and finished to remove excess material, smooth edges, and prepare mating surfaces for assembly. Clear coating and polishing improved the surface finish, while clamps held components in alignment during bonding and assembly. Fit checks confirmed the alignment of panels, wing elements, and mounting interfaces.

For the rear wing, I modeled an endplate stencil in SolidWorks within the complete rear-wing assembly, establishing the relationship between the endplate profile, element positions, and attachment points, then laser-cut its segments for welded assembly by the team. The stencil transferred the complex endplate outline and mounting-hole locations onto the carbon panel, preserving alignment with the internal mounting ribs of each wing element. Additional mounting holes provided an alternate setting: the two rearmost elements could pivot about their rear mounting holes and be secured through secondary holes at a −1° angle of attack, intended as a low-drag setting for the acceleration event with the S1223 wing elements.

VU18 Front Wing Design

Overview

For the 2027 car, I am designing the VU18 front wing in SolidWorks. My work focuses on positioning the airfoil elements for flow attachment and conditioning the inlet flow to the undertray, while staying within the 2027 FSAE IC aerodynamic envelope and integrating with the full-car assembly.

I collaborate with the senior capstone engineers on Formula SAE aero development, using Claude-assisted CFD workflows to guide SolidWorks design iterations. Front-wing CFD runs are still ahead.

Preliminary Design: Built for Meshability and Iteration

The wing shown here is a preliminary version, designed around meshability and parametrized for iteration. Each element is built from SolidWorks blocks for scalable, rotatable S1223 and NACA 2412 airfoils with defined chord lengths and defined leading and trailing edges. 3D lofts are coincident to those defined points, and the geometry avoids sliver faces and infinitely small features that would keep a simulation from converging. It is kept simple so more elements can be added later.

The wing is designed within the FSAE bounding box and around integration with the nosecone. Reference planes set a 1 in ground clearance when the wing is placed in the frame file and mated to the real ground plane, and outboard height is limited to 250 mm from the ground.

Next, I plan to add more elements inboard to use the inboard space for more aerodynamic performance, a decision that is waiting on the final front suspension geometry. I am also considering the sidepod inlet position and attitude for maximum airflow for cooling.
