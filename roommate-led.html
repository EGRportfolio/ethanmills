Roommate LED Alert System

A light box that shows college roommates what's happening inside the dorm, whether someone is sleeping, studying, or on a call, before anyone walks in. I led the hardware: the enclosure design in SolidWorks and its fabrication.

Roommate LED Alert System

Overview

College students who share a dorm need a quick, undisruptive way to know what their roommate is doing before they walk in. Coming in unexpectedly while a roommate is sleeping, studying, or on a call can lead to unnecessary disruptions and even tension.

Our objective was a roommate alert system for dorm rooms. In a survey of 26 students, 88.5% said they sometimes have to enter their dorm late at night (past 1 a.m.), and only 11.5% said they and their roommate always text each other whenever they're in the room.

We sketched four concepts, from a light box outside the door to a lighting system above it, and scored them in a weighted decision matrix against our constraints: the light had to respond in under 2 seconds, be safe, easy to maintain and install (within an hour), light enough to mount on a wall (under 1 lb), and usable by every resident. The final design is a light box with color-coded buttons that set a status light, so whoever is about to walk in can see what's happening first.

Hardware

I led the hardware. I designed the light box enclosure in SolidWorks as a 3D-printed housing with a sliding lid and holes for the buttons and wires, sized to stay light enough to mount on a wall.

The faceplate holds four color-coded push buttons, and the lid slides off so the electronics inside stay easy to reach and maintain, one of our design constraints.

Electronics

The circuit was laid out first in TinkerCAD, then built on a breadboard with an Arduino microcontroller, wiring the four push buttons to the status light with a target of under 2 seconds from button press to light.

Everything had to fit inside the enclosure, so the hardware and electronics were designed together, with the breadboard and wiring routed through the housing and faceplate. We demoed the working prototype alongside our poster.
