Project 02 · ME Analysis & Design

Beetlebot

A group project for my ME Analysis & Design class: designing and building a 3 lb combat robot, then battling it against other teams' robots. My team of three is developing a working prototype.

Course ME Analysis & Design, Villanova University Team 3 students Timeline Fall 2026 – Present
Combat Robot Prototype

Overview

My team of three has begun developing a working prototype of our combat robot. The interior electronics are soldered together with the motors and batteries, and a preliminary 3D-printed housing is built to take the exterior aluminum shell.

Our design goal is a lightweight, maneuverable beetlebot with a zero turn radius, maximum power output and torque through the wheels, and easy serviceability, all within a 3 lb limit on the full assembly.

Electronics

We soldered the interior electronic components together with the drive motors and batteries, bringing the power and drive systems up as one working unit before they go into the housing.

3D-Printed Housing

The preliminary housing was printed on a Bambu Lab X1 Carbon at 85% infill with a gyroid pattern, which keeps the part stiff and strong in every direction. The housing walls use a triangulated pattern to cut weight without giving up rigidity.

That weight work keeps the robot inside its 3 lb limit for the full assembly: it currently weighs 1.42 lb without the aluminum shell.

Aluminum Shell

The exterior shell will be cut from 1/8 in aluminum sheet stock into panels, then riveted together with brackets angled to match each joint between panels.

It mounts to the printed housing through threaded heat-set inserts, so the shell bolts on and comes off without wearing out plastic threads.

Structure & Serviceability

The layout is designed around structural integrity and serviceability: the robot has to hold up to hits and still be quick to open and repair between matches.

The motor structure is fastened in as well, keeping the drivetrain secure under impact while staying easy to reach and service.
